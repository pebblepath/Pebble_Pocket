Pebble, small enough
to carry.

A pocket-sized companion that listens for "Hey Pebble", answers in French or English in Pebble's own voice, plays the family's learning songs, calms a hard moment, and sends a hello to someone who loves them. The device stays simple; PebblePath's cloud does the thinking.

Three rules that shape every decision

The Pocket is a thin client

It captures audio, shows a screen, plays sound and reads a button. Everything that needs a brain (speech, translation, Pebble's personality) lives in the cloud, so the device firmware barely changes when the classroom needs change.

The phone is the remote, not the brain

The PebblePath app (or a browser) is where a parent uploads songs, approves contacts and picks the day's mode. The Pocket talks to the cloud directly over Wi-Fi, so it keeps working when the phone is in a bag.

Offline never feels broken

Songs and calm clips are copied to the microSD card, so Learn and Calm work with no network at all. Only Translate and Hello need a connection, and the screen says so gently.

How it fits together

live conversation path (Wi-Fi) background sync Hello message setup and admin

The Pocket never holds an API key for Claude, a speech provider or a contact's phone number. It holds one device key that identifies it to PebblePath's cloud, the same way the iOS app holds a user session. Everything sensitive stays server-side and can be rotated without touching the device.

The four things it does

Each one, end to end

Translate

"Hey Pebble, comment on dit papillon ?"

Wake word or a single press. The Pocket streams what it hears; the cloud detects the language, asks Claude as Pebble, and streams the answer back in Pebble's voice while the words appear on screen.

1Listen. Ring pulses teal; the mics are open for up to 8 seconds or until silence. 2Think. Speech-to-text (French or English, auto) → Claude with Pebble's prompt and the child's age. 3Answer. Text-to-speech in Pebble's voice, first audio within about a second; screen shows both languages. 4Again? A tap repeats it slowly. Silence after 6 seconds returns to idle.

Learn

Your songs and videos, on the wrist

A parent drops audio or video into the PebblePath app. The cloud converts it to a Pocket-friendly format, and the Pocket copies it to microSD next time it charges. A scrollable selector on the face lists them; a tap plays.

1Upload. Any audio or video from the phone, browser or a shared link. 2Prepare. Audio to 48 kHz Opus; video to 410 × 502 at 15 fps with the sound track split out. 3Sync. Manifest diff on charge; nothing is streamed live, so playback is instant and offline. 4Choose. Selector shows a cover, a title, and a duration; long titles scroll.
Calm

A gallery for the hard minutes

Raindrops on a window, a slow tide, drifting dandelion seeds. Short loops that ask nothing of a child, for when a moment has gone sideways or when everyone needs to stay still for a photo. Chosen by the parent, played by the child.

1Curate. A starter set ships in firmware; parents add their own loops through the app. 2Open. Swipe left from idle; large tiles, one per loop, no text needed. 3Play. Full-face loop with a soft sound bed; brightness eases down after 30 seconds. 4Leave. Any button press returns to idle. No timers, no rewards.
Hello

Press and hold to send a hello

Hold the side button for a second and a half; a ring fills while the child talks. Let go and the clip goes to the approved contacts only. Grandparents get a notification from the PebblePath app and hear it there. No open messaging, no replies to the device in v0.1.

1Hold. Haptic tick at 1.5 seconds, ring starts filling, 20-second cap. 2Release. Clip is stored and the face shows "Sent to Mamie and Papi" with a small wave. 3Deliver. Cloud pushes to approved contacts' PebblePath app; falls back to an SMS link that expires. 4Trust. Contacts are added and removed in the app by a parent, never on the device.
Known constraints, decided up front

What the hardware makes us do

Build the audio path on ESP-IDF, not Arduino

The ES8311 codec and the ES7210 echo canceller share an I2S clock. Full duplex works when the power rails come up first, then the I2S master clock, then both codecs as slaves. Arduino examples hide that order; ESP-IDF exposes it.

Assume about an hour of screen-on time

The 400 mAh cell gives roughly an hour at full brightness and three to four with the screen off. The face dims after 10 seconds, sleeps after 30, and wakes on button or wrist raise. Learn and Calm run with the screen at 40 percent.

No Bluetooth speaker mode

The ESP32-S3 has Bluetooth LE only, so the iPhone cannot treat the Pocket as a speaker. Media reaches the Pocket through the cloud sync instead, which is why the microSD card exists in this design.

One watt of speaker

Loud enough for one child at arm's length, not for a room. Pebble speaks close and slow. A future tabletop dock could add a proper speaker over the reserved I2C and UART pads.

Wake word comes second

Espressif's ESP-SR runs wake words on-device, but a custom "Hey Pebble" model has to be trained through their service. v0.1 ships button-first with the stock wake word for testing; "Hey Pebble" lands in v0.2.

Privacy by construction

Audio only leaves the device while the ring is pulsing. Nothing is recorded in idle. Transcripts live under the child's PebblePath profile with the same retention as chat, and a parent can wipe the device from the app.

Build plan

Five short phases

Each phase ends with something a child can hold. Nothing here needs the phone app to be finished; the Portal in a browser is enough for the parent side until the iOS work lands.

Phase 0week of arrival

Hello, screen

Flash the Waveshare demo, then a bare ESP-IDF project that draws the idle face in LVGL, reads the buttons, and joins Wi-Fi from a hard-coded config. Confirm full-duplex audio with a beep-and-record test.

Phase 12 weeks

Translate, button-first

Cloud Function opens a WebSocket per device. Pocket streams Opus on press; cloud runs speech-to-text, calls Claude with the Pebble prompt, streams text-to-speech back. Screen shows both languages. This is the moment the device becomes Pebble.

Phase 21 week

Learn and Calm from microSD

Media upload in the Portal, transcode in a Function, manifest sync on charge, selector and gallery on the face. Ship a starter set of three calm loops inside the firmware so Calm works on day one.

Phase 31 week

Hello to approved contacts

Hold-to-record with the filling ring, upload, contact approval in the Portal, push to the PebblePath app with an expiring link fallback. Parents see every Hello in the day view.

Phase 4ongoing

"Hey Pebble", OTA, and the day view

Train the custom wake word, add over-the-air firmware so the classroom never needs a cable, and surface the Pocket's day (what was asked, what played, battery) in the app next to the child's other PebblePath signals.

Suggested repo layout

firmware/ESP-IDF project: LVGL face, audio pipeline, WebSocket client, microSD sync, OTA.
cloud/Firebase Cloud Functions: pocket session, media service, hello delivery, fleet. Reuses the existing PebblePath Firebase project.
portal/Parent-side screens for the Pocket inside the existing Portal (Lit): pair, media, contacts, day view.
docs/These pages. index.html is the architecture, screens.html the face mockups. Served by GitHub Pages.
