How it's held

The face knows which way is up

The motion sensor only makes the Pocket easier to use. It never becomes something a child has to do: no shaking, no tilting to trigger anything. The buttons stay the way to act.

Lifted on the lanyardThe cord runs through the top slot, so when a child lifts the Pocket to their eyes the case is upside down. The face turns itself over and reads the right way for them.
On the tableLying flat, the face keeps the way it was last facing, so nothing turns under a finger mid-tap. Raise it to wake it.

Face-down: asleepTurned over, the screen and microphone are off, so nothing can be recorded. Holding the top button (BOOT) does nothing, so there are no pocket hellos. Pick it up to wake it.
Icon set

Seventeen shapes, one weight

Every icon is a single filled or 2.4-stroke silhouette on a 24 grid with round caps, no inner detail, so it still reads at 88 px on the panel and at 44 px as a hint (44 and 22 px in these half-size mockups). The ripple stone is the only "character". Nothing has a face.

Face rules for ages three and up

How it stays usable by someone who cannot read

One shape per face

A single centred object tells the child what this face is for. If a state needs two things on screen, one of them is spoken instead.

Words only when teaching

The only text on any child-facing face is the pair Pebble is teaching. Status, names, titles and errors are all spoken by Pebble or shown to the parent in the app. The one exception is the small clock on Home, which is for the grown-up.

Colour is the label

Teal glow: Pebble is listening or talking. Amber: the French word. Terracotta: a hello for family. Parents choose a colour per song, so tiles never need titles.

Big targets, no precision

Every tappable thing is at least 96 px at 1×, which is 7.7 mm on this panel (112 px if we want 9 mm). Tiles fill the face in twos. Nothing sits in a corner. Damp thumbs and a bouncing lap still work.

Buttons are the safety net

Top button (BOOT): press to talk, hold for hello. While a song plays, a press pauses it. Bottom button (PWR): wake, and home from anywhere. Swipes go left and right only, so a child who gets lost is one press from the stone.

Nothing to finish, nothing to win

No streaks, stars or timers. A song ends and the tile comes back. A calm loop runs until someone presses. The device never asks the child for anything.
